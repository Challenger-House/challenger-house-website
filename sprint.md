**lead:** The 1-Day opens the door. The Sprint is where capability and results compound: three months of work wrapped around a three-day Residency, so the new way of working doesn't just get experienced, it gets embedded.

## The arc (three phases; middle is the emphasised "core")

### Before · weeks 1 to 4 — Define
We define the use cases with you, build the story, map the stakeholders, and work 1:1 with every participant at the Drawing Board. People tell us individually what they would never say in front of peers and boss, and that becomes the diagnostic data the room runs on. The Residency only works because of what happens before it.

### The core · 3 days — The Residency
Your team, in one place, on your real problems and your real data. A month's work in three days, and somewhere in the middle the realisation that the speed isn't the point.

### After · weeks 5 to 12 — Embed
Office hours, masterclasses, one-to-ones, ambassador roles, weekly rhythms, and the measurement of what actually changed. Adoption is what happens after the room.

## What clients walk away with

**h2:** Exemplary deliverables from previous Sprints.

Six deliverables, arrow-marked, two columns:

1. **Three priority workflows** identified, rebuilt and running with AI inside them, chosen by the team, not by us.
2. **Handover packages** accelerated from months to days, with the receiving side pressure-testing them in the room.
3. **A clickable KPI dashboard mockup** built by the team itself, interfacing with their existing tooling.
4. **3 to 5x reported speed** on research, drafts and mockups, and a backlog cleared that had been immovable for months.
5. **Self-organised ambassador roles** and a weekly sync the team set up without being asked. Adoption taking root, not being rolled out.
6. **Board-ready artefacts**: working demos, cases, maps and multimedia, not proposals about what could be built.

## Why residential

**h2:** AI gives you speed. The house gives you depth.

Because AI compresses screen work so effectively, the human work can finally expand. People eat together, argue over the artefacts their AI creates, take walks between sessions, have the conversation at 10pm that changes the project direction. This is not a workshop where people sit around tables. It is a house. You come here to challenge yourself, challenge each other, and challenge the way you have been working.

**compound line (left-aligned here):** AI adoption doesn't compound in individuals. *It compounds in teams.*

**small quote:**
> After the Residency, the group self-organised its next steps without being asked: ambassador roles in their home teams, and a weekly sync they set up themselves. That is not a typical result. That is adoption taking root.
