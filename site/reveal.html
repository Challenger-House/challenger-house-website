Zone 02 / Build the missing middle

Three things to build, so it doesn't depend on heroes.

Advanced · for your leadership team

Automation runs on your formal organisation: processes, documents, data. Augmentation runs on the informal one: judgement, trust, how people really decide. The bridge between the two is missing. Leaders build it with three things. Each kit gets you a first draft, not a finished answer. The real work happens when your leadership team discusses it.

Kit 01 · Constitution · Contains the fear

Write your AI constitution.

Not governance. Not sixty pages. The CEO’s why, what AI is for, what it is never for, and what stays human.

What it does
Drafts a one-page AI constitution: the CEO’s manifesto plus clear clauses your people can trust.
When to use it
Before, or right at the start of, any serious AI rollout. Start with the CEO or managing director.
What you need
Your company type, size and regulated context. Optionally your strategy or values document.
Time
20 minutes for the draft, then one leadership-team session to make it yours.
What you get
A one-page draft with two key clauses built in, plus five questions your leadership team must answer.
Watch out
A draft is not a constitution. Your leadership team writes the final version together, or nobody will believe it.
The prompt 
Help me draft a one-page AI constitution for my organisation: [company type, size, regulated context]. Use this structure: 1) The manifesto: three to five sentences from our CEO on why we use AI, in plain language. 2) What we use AI for. 3) What we never use AI for. 4) What stays human, with named decisions. 5) How we handle mistakes. Include these two clauses word for word: “Meeting transcripts are never used for performance evaluation.” and “Final release of a submission stays with a named person.” Add an AI amnesty: people can bring in AI tools they used on private accounts, with no penalty. Write it so anyone can read it in three minutes. Then list five questions our leadership team must answer before we sign it.
Kit 02 · Transcripts · The hardest adaptive work

Make meeting transcripts a habit.

Transcripts encode your informal organisation, so you can see the gap between how you say you work and how you do. It only works with high psychological safety.

What it does
Sets up consent, ground rules and a four-week pilot for transcribing leadership-team meetings.
When to use it
After the constitution. Start with the leadership team, never with the people below you.
What you need
A transcription tool your organisation has approved, and the consent of everyone in the meeting.
Time
30 minutes to prepare, then one meeting a week for a month.
What you get
A consent text, five ground rules, a pilot plan, and the questions for your works council and data protection officer.
Watch out
This is not legal advice. In Germany, bring in the works council and data protection early. Read for patterns, never for people.
The prompt 
We want to start transcribing our leadership team’s meetings, so we can learn how we really work and decide. We are [company type, country, regulated context]. Draft: 1) a short, plain consent text for participants; 2) five ground rules: who can read transcripts, how long they are kept, what they are never used for, and how anyone can pause the recording; 3) a four-week pilot plan, starting with one meeting a week; 4) the three questions we must agree with our works council and data protection officer before we start. Keep everything short and in plain language.
Kit 03 · Incentives · Skin in the game

Make AI contribution visible, and fairly rewarded.

“What’s in it for me? That you can fire me faster?” Answer that question before your people ask it.

What it does
Turns the AI work in your team into a simple register, and suggests fair ways to recognise the people who built it.
When to use it
Once a quarter, or as soon as people start building prompts, templates or agents others use.
What you need
A list of the AI assets your team uses (prompts, templates, agents, automations) and who built them.
Time
About 15 minutes.
What you get
An asset register, three options to recognise the builders, the risks of each, and a paragraph for your next team meeting.
Watch out
Start with recognition, not money. A promise you cannot keep destroys more trust than no promise.
The prompt 
Help me make the AI work in my team visible and fairly recognised. Here is what I know about the AI assets we use: [list prompts, templates, agents, automations, and who built each one]. 1) Turn this into a simple register: asset, builder, who uses it, estimated time saved per month. 2) Suggest three ways to recognise the builders, from public praise to a share in the value created. 3) Name the risks of each option. 4) Draft one short paragraph I could say in our next team meeting.

Make AI use visible, leaders first. One slide at the end of every presentation and meeting. What one person learns, the whole room learns. Then praise it in public.

Eight lenses for floor three

Gareth Morgan showed that every way of seeing an organisation is a metaphor. Each one shows something and hides something. Tap any lens to open it.

LENS 01MachineWhat runs like clockwork, and what can't?Open +

LOOKS ATProcess, roles, control, KPIs, compliance.

REVEALSWhere work is standardised, measurable and efficient.

HIDESPeople, meaning, and everything that doesn't fit the process.

WHAT AI AMPLIFIESEfficiency, and rigidity. A faster machine is still a machine.

LENS 02OrganismWhat is our environment forcing us to become?Open +

LOOKS ATAdaptation, environmental fit, resilience, survival.

REVEALSThe pressures from outside and how well you fit them.

HIDESChoice and power. Organisations do more than react.

WHAT AI AMPLIFIESThe speed of sensing, if anyone is listening.

LENS 03BrainWhere is knowledge trapped?Open +

LOOKS ATLearning, feedback loops, memory, information flow.

REVEALSWhere knowledge flows, and where it is trapped in heads and silos.

HIDESWho decides what gets learned and what gets forgotten.

WHAT AI AMPLIFIESLearning loops, or the same mistake, faster.

LENS 04CultureWhat do we really value when nobody's watching?Open +

LOOKS ATShared meaning, stories, rituals, language, norms.

REVEALSWhat people really value, beyond what hangs on the wall.

HIDESHow culture can also be used to control.

LENS 05Political systemWho really decides?Open +

LOOKS ATInterests, alliances, conflict, power.

REVEALSWho decides, who can move things and who can block them.

HIDESShared purpose. Not everything is a game.

WHAT AI AMPLIFIESWhoever controls the data, the tools and the prompts.

LENS 06Psychic prisonWhich rule does nobody question?Open +

LOOKS ATUnconscious assumptions, inherited beliefs, identity.

And one question underneath all eight

Ronald Heifetz and Marty Linsky · Adaptive leadershipTechnical problem or adaptive challenge?Get off the dance floor. Go up to the balcony.

Technical problems can be solved with expertise, a tool or a decision. Licences, access, training.

Adaptive challenges need people to change habits, relationships, assumptions or identity. No expert can solve them for you, because the people with the problem have to change.

The most common mistake is a technical fix for an adaptive challenge. Another tool rollout, when the real issue is who gets to decide.

Balcony and dance floor. On the dance floor you act, decide and talk. On the balcony you step back and see the pattern. The best leaders move between the two. The mirror is a balcony you can visit any time.

Resistance is data. When people push back, ask what loss they are protecting: status, expertise, certainty, relationships, control.

What AI changes: rolling out AI is the technical part. Using it to change how you lead and decide is the adaptive part, and that is where the value sits.

Zone 04 / The Org Mirror: build it

Want to do this properly? Here is everything.

Advanced · about 15 minutes to set up · free

Point the mirror at your own team or unit, with all the lenses, evidence and salience. We give the whole method away. Open the steps in order.

Step 1Before you startOne question, three to ten sources, and one rule.
  1. Pick one question. Not "how is our culture?" but "why do our AI pilots stall after they work?"
  2. Collect three to ten sources. Real conversation is best: leadership meeting transcripts, workshop notes, retros. Add a few documents for context.
  3. Get consent for transcripts. Everyone in the meeting should know their words will be read this way, and why.
  4. Use an approved tool. Meeting transcripts do not belong in a personal account.
The one rule

Transcripts are never used for performance evaluation. Say it out loud before you start. Without it, nobody speaks honestly in front of a microphone again, and the mirror goes blind.

The mirror keeps itself honest in three ways. It separates evidence from interpretation. It names the keyhole: whose voices are missing from what it read. And it describes systems, never anyone's personality or performance.

# Lens 1, Gareth Morgan, Images of Organization
Eight metaphors, each a different and always incomplete view of the same organisation: machine (process, control), organism (adaptation), brain (learning, knowledge), culture (norms, stories), political system (power, interests), psychic prison (stuck assumptions), flux and transformation (emergence, paradox), instrument of domination (unequal influence, hidden burdens). # Lens 2, Ronald Heifetz and Marty Linsky, adaptive leadership
Technical work can be solved with knowledge, a decision, a process or a tool. Adaptive work needs people to change habits, relationships, responsibility, identity or assumptions. Most important: misdiagnoses, where a technical answer is applied to an adaptive challenge. For each give the apparent problem, the solution being tried and the adaptive challenge underneath, as a hypothesis. Balcony means stepping out of the day to see patterns. Dance floor means acting, deciding and talking. Watch for fear of loss, work avoidance, too little or too much tension, and whether there are spaces where conflict can be worked. Treat resistance as data: what loss is it protecting?

# AI as mirror and amplifier
Which unwritten rules, knowledge gaps, contradictions or power effects become visible through the material? If AI made this work much faster, what would get amplified, for better and for worse? Do not confuse visibility with certainty.

# Salience
For each pattern give a salience of HIGH, MEDIUM or LOW: how often and how strongly it shows up in the sources, and how much it matters for the stated focus. Salience rates a pattern, never the organisation or a person. No maturity levels, scores or rankings of people.

# Answer form
Answer the actual question first. For short questions: at most three strong patterns, each with salience, evidence, interpretation, counter-reading and one next question. For a full mirror: 1 brief, period and boundary; 2 sources used and the keyhole; 3 core thesis in a few lines; 4 Morgan, ordered by explanatory power; 5 Heifetz, technical versus adaptive and the misdiagnoses; 6 what AI would amplify; 7 the three most useful lenses for a leadership conversation now; 8 counter-readings and open checks. If a source is missing, say so and shrink the claim. Explain each lens in one plain sentence the first time it appears. End a first run with questions and hypotheses, not general recommendations.

For each pattern give a salience of HIGH, MEDIUM or LOW: how often and how strongly it shows up in the sources, and how much it matters for the stated focus. Salience rates a pattern, never the organisation or a person. No maturity levels, scores or rankings of people.

# Answer form
Answer the actual question first. For short questions: at most three strong patterns, each with salience, evidence, interpretation, counter-reading and one next question. For a full mirror: 1 brief, period and boundary; 2 sources used and the keyhole; 3 core thesis in a few lines; 4 Morgan, ordered by explanatory power; 5 Heifetz, technical versus adaptive and the misdiagnoses; 6 what AI would amplify; 7 the three most useful lenses for a leadership conversation now; 8 counter-readings and open checks. If a source is missing, say so and shrink the claim. Explain each lens in one plain sentence the first time it appears. End a first run with questions and hypotheses, not general recommendations.

The point of the whole exercise.
