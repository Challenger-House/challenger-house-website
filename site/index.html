The imagination gap.

We see it in every leadership room: the blocker isn’t the technology, and it isn’t resistance. It’s imagination. People cannot ask AI for what they cannot imagine, and every day spent inside Copilot’s walls, the gap between what’s possible and what you can picture gets wider.

Zone 05 / Our point of view

Three things we hold to be true.

P.01

POV 01You have to experience AI to understand it.

Reading and talking about AI is knowing that. Leading with it demands three other kinds of knowing, and they only arrive through doing, on your own work, with someone beside you.

This is why we coach rather than train. A training room transfers knowing-that to twenty people at once, and knowing-that is precisely what AI is making free. The other three arrive one person at a time, through undivided attention on a real problem. Do that regularly and it compounds. Sit through another course and almost none of it survives Monday.

Knowing that Knowing how Knowing what matters Knowing through co-creation

The four ways of knowing, and the day built on them →

P.02

POV 02You need a WHY that goes beyond productivity.

Efficiency gains are table stakes. The real why has to be specific to your organisation and central to your strategy, and it cannot be delegated. The original thinking is CEO work, a rallying cry, a manifesto, and eventually a constitution.

The AI Constitution →

P.03

POV 03AI will stress-test your foundations.

Your organisational design, your ways of working, and eventually the employer-employee contract itself. AI runs on your tacit knowledge. But why would your people write down the very know-how that makes them hard to replace, so it can be automated? They won’t, and they shouldn’t, not for free. We advocate skin in the game, on both sides: contribution recorded, recognised, rewarded.
